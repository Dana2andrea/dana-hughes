The problem

The documentation tax is paid in instructional time.

Every learning period, California homeschool families and the education specialists who support them have to prove what each student did. Before the charter will approve a curriculum purchase, count the work toward enrollment, or sign off on progress, every nature walk, every read-aloud, every kitchen-table fraction lesson has to be matched to the right California state standard — and the matches have to be defensible.

The process forces families and teachers into one of two bad options:

Stick to boxed curriculum that documents itself, or use hands-on, experiential learning and lose the instructional hours every week that should belong to the student.

Most pick the first option not because it's better for the child, but because it's the only one that doesn't cost them the lesson. CA Homeschool Align closes that gap — documentation that travels with the activity, not after it.

Why this matters

The kids who need this most are the ones losing out.

Hands-on, experiential learning is not a preference — it is measurably more effective, and the research has been consistent for decades. The largest recent body of evidence comes from a 2022 systematic review published in Frontiers in Pediatrics, which synthesized findings across dozens of controlled studies of school-aged children.

A 2022 systematic review found that learning through movement — the kind of hands-on, experiential work the app is designed to document — improves both attention and academic performance. Source: Frontiers in Pediatrics (2022), systematic review of physically active learning interventions.

When documentation overhead pushes families and teachers toward worksheets, the students who lose are the ones who learn best by doing — and that's a lot of students. The app is the documentation layer that lets families and Education Specialists say yes to the better lesson, with the compliance evidence already in hand when the Learning Period meeting arrives.

What it does

A photo, a sentence, a measurable record of progress.

The whole product is built around one loop: a family or teacher describes (or photographs) something a student did, the app returns the standards it aligns to with plain-language reasoning, and the educator reviews and approves the matches that fit. Over a Learning Period, those approved matches become a running, measurable record of student progress against California state standards — exportable as a charter-ready PDF the moment it's needed.

You are always in control of the student vault. Add the matches that align to your lessons; skip the ones that don't. Nothing leaves your account.

01 · Capture

Photo or sentence in, standards out.

Snap a picture of a tide pool, type "we baked sourdough and tripled the recipe," or both. Google's Gemini API reads the evidence and returns the California standards (CCSS, NGSS, HSS) it actually maps to — documentation that travels with the lesson, not after it.

02 · Translate

The Academic Hook.

Every match comes with a one-sentence translation of the dry framework language into something a family or teacher can actually say at a learning period meeting. We call it "the academic hook." It is the line that turns a sticky-fingered afternoon into a defensible standards conversation.

03 · Grade-appropriate by design

Family-style, with guardrails.

Pick up to three grade levels at once for family-style learning across siblings, or for an education specialist supporting a multi-grade household. The match logic only surfaces standards at each child's grade level — wrong-grade matches are gated, so the record stays clean and charter-defensible.

04 · The Progress Portrait

A running, measurable record per student.

Approved matches accumulate into a learning-period progress view, filterable by subject, framework, and date — and exportable as a designed PDF for charter approval. Zero-reporting guarantee: the record is private to the family or teacher and never shared with any charter, district, or agency.

Step 01 · Capture

The input matches how families and teachers think about a day.

Four ordered steps — target student, subject focus, up to three grade levels, then a single field for the activity itself. The grade selector explicitly accommodates family-style learning across siblings; the matcher only ever surfaces standards at the chosen levels, so the record stays charter-defensible.

The capture box does not require academic language. It is meant to be written the way the day actually happened — in the moment, on a phone, while the student is still in earshot. The app handles the translation to framework language on the next screen.

Step 02 · Match & translate

Every standard comes with its translation built in.

Returned standards are tagged by subject, grade, and framework (CCSS, NGSS, HSS) — and each one carries an "Academic Hook." One sentence that turns "3-LS4-4 — make a claim about the merit of a solution" into language a family or teacher can use at the Learning Period meeting without a script.

Adding a match to the student's record is one tap, and only after a parent or teacher has read and accepted the reasoning. The educator is always the editor of record — the AI just drafts.

Step 03 · Track

A measurable record of progress, one student at a time.

Each approved match becomes a dated record tied to a student and filterable by learning period, subject, and framework. Over the school year, those records compose into a running, measurable picture of where the student is growing — not a binder of one-off worksheets.

The vault is also where the zero-reporting guarantee lives. Data is private to the family or teacher and never shared with any charter or agency. The "Confidential Parent Record" badge is not decorative — exports are user-initiated only, and the educator decides what leaves the building.

Step 04 · Export

The charter wants paper. The educator decides exactly what's on it.

The export modal makes the "what leaves the building" decision explicit. Filter by learning period, choose whether to include the AI's match logic, and pick the format the Education Specialist actually wants — a designed PDF for the meeting, or a CSV for longitudinal tracking. The sovereignty guarantee is restated at the moment of export, on purpose.

Everything is user-initiated. Nothing is reported automatically. The educator decides who sees the record — the app's job is to make the record itself worth sending.

Built for trust

Quality and safety are not features. They are the foundation.

California families and the education specialists supporting them are trusting this tool with their students' learning records and, by extension, with their charter compliance. That responsibility shapes every choice — from what the AI is allowed to do, to where the data lives, to who is qualified to author the matching logic in the first place.

Privacy commitment

Zero-reporting guarantee.

The student record is private to the family or teacher who created it. No charter, school, district, or agency receives data from this app — ever. Exports are user-initiated only. The educator decides who sees the record.

AI commitment

AI assists. The educator decides.

Every match is presented with its reasoning shown. You stay in control of the student vault — add the matches that align to your lessons, skip the ones that don't. The Academic Hook is a draft you read, edit, and approve — not a verdict the app imposes on the progress record.

Accuracy commitment

Grade-appropriate by design.

The matching logic only surfaces standards at the student's grade level. Wrong-grade matches are explicitly gated, so progress against standards stays measurable, honest, and defensible the moment the PDF lands in the Education Specialist's inbox.

Authorship commitment

Written by a credentialed educator.

Not a tech team guessing at what teachers need. Authored by a credentialed CA TK–12 educator with 15+ years of classroom and curriculum experience, currently serving as Educational Coordinator inside a California virtual charter homeschool school.

Under the hood

A lean stack chosen for one person to ship and maintain.

What's next

From documentation after the lesson to documentation inside it.

The next big move is closing the loop the other way — instead of writing up what already happened, the app generates the work sample itself, in the moment, using the same matching logic in reverse. Compliance evidence that is produced by the lesson, not after it.

Shipping now

Instant work sample generation.

Auto-populated templates pulling from the same matching logic — with photo upload areas, grade-appropriate reflection prompts, and built-in multiple-choice questions. The student's work becomes its own evidence, the moment it happens, without taking a single instructional minute away from the lesson.

Next quarter

Stripe payments & charter tier.

Currently invite-and-access-code based. Stripe-backed monthly and yearly plans for families, plus a charter-organization tier for education specialists who want to share match logic across the families they support.

On the roadmap

Beyond California.

The matching logic is state-agnostic; the standards library is the work. Expanding the standards corpus to neighboring states with strong charter homeschool networks (Texas, Florida, Arizona) is the obvious next move.
